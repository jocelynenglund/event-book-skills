Event Book

Your event model, as an e-book.

Sketch how a system works on your phone, as an event model, and share it as a picture. It's a way to show people how simple an event-sourced system really is.

Reading a board

Time flows left to right. Each column is a slice, one small step of the story, and three lanes run through them:

  • UI · automation: what a person sees, or a processor that does the work
  • Commands · views: what someone asks for, and the read models built for them
  • Events: the facts that happened, the source of truth
  • State change: screen → command → event
  • State view: events → read model → screen
  • The system reacts: a processor works through a to-do list and sends a command

Tips

  • Sketch the journey in the outline: ＋ Do, ＋ See and ＋ Auto under each chapter, and tap a step to change it.
  • Group slices into chapters, and fold a chapter into a book spine.
  • Add Given / When / Then scenarios; the GWT lane under the events shows them.
  • Share the whole model, one chapter, or 4:5 slides for a carousel.
  • Install it from your browser's menu to keep it on your home screen.

Bookshelves and library

Your bookshelves are the models on this device. A shelf you keep on the server (☁) is also in your library, so your other devices get it. A share link uploads a copy of that one model so others can open it.

A frustrated writer's notebook

I'm a frustrated writer who ended up modeling software on the train. Writers argue about whether they're plotters, who outline the story first, or pantsers, who write by the seat of their pants and find it as they go. Modeling works the same way, so Event Book asks which you are: 📋 plotters start in the outline, 🌀 pantsers straight on the board. Either way your models end up on a bookshelf, one book per chapter.

It began as eventPAD, for Personal Architecture Diagrams. A model written in chapters, kept on shelves and published in editions turned out to be a book, so now it’s Event Book.
